Age at the Nobel Prize: Literature vs. Other Categories

Author

Esra Dogan

Approach

Motivation

The Nobel Prize in Literature is often described as a reward for a lifetime of work. In this assignment, I want to see what the data says about that: at what age do writers receive the prize, and how does this compare to the other Nobel categories?

Questions

  1. On average, at what age do laureates win the Nobel Prize in Literature?
  2. Has the age of Literature laureates changed over the decades?
  3. Are Literature laureates older than laureates in other categories?
  4. Who are the youngest and oldest laureates in each category?

Steps

  1. Use the Nobel Prize API v2.1 laureates endpoint (https://api.nobelprize.org/2.1/laureates) and request all laureates in JSON format with httr2.
  2. Convert the JSON response into a tibble. Each laureate has a list of prizes (nobelPrizes), so I will use unnest_longer() to get one row per prize.
  3. Keep only the columns I need: name, birth date, award year and category. I will take the birth year from the birth date.
  4. Calculate age at the time of the award (award year minus birth year).
  5. Answer each question with a simple table or plot.

Anticipated Challenges

  • Organizations (such as the Red Cross) are also listed as laureates but have no birth date, so I will filter them out.
  • Some laureates have won more than one prize (such as Marie Curie), so they will appear in more than one row after unnesting.
  • The API returns a limited number of results per request by default, so I will set a higher limit (or use offset) to make sure I get all laureates.
  • Some birth dates may be missing or incomplete, so I will only use the year.

The JSON data is nested, so I used hoist() to pull out only the fields I need: name, gender, birth date and prizes. Because some laureates have won more than one prize, I used unnest_longer() to get one row per prize, and then extracted the award year and category.

Organizations have no birth date, so I removed them. Some birth dates are incomplete (for example, “1948-00-00”), so I only used the birth year. Age is calculated as award year minus birth year.

laureates_age <- laureates |>
  filter(!is.na(birth_date)) |>
  mutate(
    birth_year = as.integer(str_sub(birth_date, 1, 4)),
    award_year = as.integer(award_year),
    age        = award_year - birth_year
  ) |>
  filter(!is.na(age))

Question 1: On average, at what age do laureates win the Nobel Prize in Literature?

literature <- laureates_age |>
  filter(category == "Literature")

literature |>
  summarise(
    laureates  = n(),
    mean_age   = round(mean(age), 1),
    median_age = median(age),
    youngest   = min(age),
    oldest     = max(age)
  )

Literature laureates receive the prize at an average age of 65 (median 67). The youngest was Rudyard Kipling at 42 (1907) and the oldest was Doris Lessing at 88 (2007). This supports the idea that the prize usually comes late in a writer’s career.

Question 2: Has the age of Literature laureates changed over the decades?

ggplot(literature, aes(x = award_year, y = age)) +

  labs(title = "Age of Literature laureates at the time of the award",
       x = "Award year", y = "Age") +
  theme_minimal()
`geom_smooth()` using formula = 'y ~ x'

Each point is one Literature laureate, and the blue line shows the overall trend. Ages vary a lot from year to year, but the trend is clear: after a dip to around 60 in the 1920s–1930s, the age of Literature laureates has steadily increased and is now around 70.

Literature laureates are among the oldest: their average age (65.0) is second only to Economic Sciences (67.2). Science laureates are younger, with Physics having the youngest average age (57.8). One possible reason is that economics and literature prizes often reward a lifetime of work.

Question 4: Who are the youngest and oldest laureates in each category?

youngest <- laureates_age |>
  group_by(category) |>
  slice_min(age, n = 1, with_ties = FALSE) |>
  mutate(type = "Youngest")

oldest <- laureates_age |>
  group_by(category) |>
  slice_max(age, n = 1, with_ties = FALSE) |>
  mutate(type = "Oldest")

Looking at the oldest laureate in each category, most of them won recently: five of the six received the prize after 1990. This supports the finding in Question 2 that laureates are getting older over time.

Conclusion

The data supports the idea that the Nobel Prize in Literature rewards a lifetime of work. Writers receive the prize at an average age of 65, older than laureates in most other categories, and the average age of Literature laureates has increased over time.
